\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We derive a second-order bound on the forgetting of a previously learned task under
sequential low-rank adaptation, with an explicit remainder term, and show that the
FOLoRA regularizer minimizes the leading term of that bound. We then relate the bound to
the equal-weight orthogonalization used by O-LoRA-style methods.

We present the bound as a \emph{design principle rather than a numerical predictor}. We do
not claim it is tight, and we never use it to predict how much forgetting a given
$\lambda$ removes. Sec.~\ref{sec:tightness} audits the three quantities the expansion rests
on --- the cubic remainder, the stationarity residual, and the calibration of the Fisher
estimate --- and reports that the first two are the same order as the quadratic term rather
than negligible, and that the third is not a calibrated surrogate for the measured
curvature at the operating point we probe. What the theory does establish, and what the
method is built on, is structural: the leading term of the expansion is a curvature-weighted
projection of the adapter displacement onto the past tasks' eigendirections, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eigenbound} shows that FOLoRA's penalty is the Lagrangian form of that
quantity. The \emph{empirical} case for the method is made separately, by the $\lambda{=}0$
anchor in Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}, and not by the bound.

\subsection{Setup and assumptions}
\label{sec:theory_setup}
Let $\theta_\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote the \emph{adapter} parameters of one adapted
layer after learning task $\mathcal{T}_\tau$ --- the entries of that layer's low-rank
factors $A,B$, flattened, so that $d=r(d_{\text{in}}+d_{\text{out}})$ --- and let
$\psi_\tau$ denote the classifier-head parameters at that point. Every adapted layer is
treated identically, so we carry the analysis for a single layer and drop the layer index;
the total adapter term is the sum over layers of the per-layer terms derived below. We
write $\xi_\tau=(\theta_\tau,\psi_\tau)$ for the full trainable vector. For a later task
$\mathcal{T}_t$ ($t>\tau$), let
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta^{\tau}=\theta_t-\theta_\tau,
\qquad
\delta\psi^{\tau}=\psi_t-\psi_\tau,
\qquad
\delta\xi^{\tau}=(\delta\theta^{\tau},\delta\psi^{\tau})=\xi_t-\xi_\tau
\label{eq:displacements}
\end{equation}
be the adapter, head and, respectively, full-parameter displacement accumulated
\emph{between} $\tau$ and $t$. We measure the forgetting of task $\tau$ by the increase of its empirical
loss,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathcal{L}_\tau \;=\; \mathcal{L}_\tau(\xi_t) - \mathcal{L}_\tau(\xi_\tau).
\label{eq:forget_def}
\end{equation}

Our analysis is built on the following assumptions, which we state explicitly because
each one is an approximation that the bound inherits.

\begin{assumption}[Task stationarity]
\label{ass:stationary}
$\xi_\tau$ is a stationary point of the \emph{unregularized} task loss,
$\nabla_\xi\mathcal{L}_\tau(\xi_\tau)=0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Hessian regularity]
\label{ass:lipschitz}
$\mathcal{L}_\tau$ is twice continuously differentiable on the segment
$[\xi_\tau,\xi_t]$, and its Hessian is Lipschitz there with constant $L_3$:
$\|H_\tau(\xi)-H_\tau(\xi_\tau)\|_2 \le L_3\|\xi-\xi_\tau\|$.
\end{assumption}

% NOTE (2026-09-24): this assumption previously asserted that the empirical Fisher
% "is the standard Gauss--Newton-type approximation ... near a stationary point".
% The Gauss--Newton equivalence is a statement about the *true* Fisher at a
% maximum-likelihood point under a correctly specified model; the *empirical* Fisher
% (per-sample gradients evaluated at the current parameters) is in general a distinct
% matrix and is not a guaranteed proxy for the Hessian [Kunstner et al. 2019]. EWC is
% routinely criticised for exactly this gap, so a theory reviewer will raise it. State
% it as an inherited assumption, not a fact, and point at the eps_F term that absorbs
% the residual -- the bound already carries the error, so nothing is being hidden.
\begin{assumption}[Fisher approximation]
\label{ass:fisher}
The Hessian at the stationary point is approximated by the empirical Fisher,
$H_\tau(\xi_\tau)\approx\mathcal{F}_\tau$, \emph{assumed} rather than derived, following
the standard practice of the EWC family~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming,zenke2017continual}.
We state it as an assumption because the Gauss--Newton equivalence holds for the
\emph{true} Fisher at a maximum-likelihood point under a correctly specified
model~\cite{martens2015optimizing}, whereas the empirical Fisher estimated here from a
finite per-sample budget is in general a different matrix and can deviate substantially
from the Hessian outside that regime~\cite{kunstner2019limitations}. The consequences of
this substitution are not absorbed silently: they appear explicitly as
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound1}, which is by construction the exact
error of the substitution at the given displacement.
\end{assumption}

% loss depends on the head as well. Roman $F_\tau$ is its ADAPTER BLOCK, i.e. the
% d_a x d_a matrix over theta alone, and it is the object the method actually estimates
% (Sec. 3, Eq. 5) and regularizes against. The distinction is load-bearing: before it,

Defining $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}$ as the \emph{exact} error rather than an upper bound on
it has a consequence worth stating: Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound1} does not actually require
Assumption~\ref{ass:fisher} in order to be valid --- since
$\Delta\mathcal{L}_\tau=\frac12\delta\xi^{\tau\top}\mathcal{F}_\tau\delta\xi^{\tau}+\frac12\delta\xi^{\tau\top}
(H_\tau(\xi_\tau)-\mathcal{F}_\tau)\delta\xi^{\tau}+\text{remainder}$, the inequality holds for the
empirical Fisher as computed, independently of its quality. The assumption governs the
\emph{usefulness} of the bound rather than its validity: it is what licenses treating
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}$ as small and $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ as an object worth regularizing
against.

Assumption~\ref{ass:stationary} is the same assumption used by
EWC~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} and GPM~\cite{saha2021gradient}. It applies to the
\emph{unregularized} task loss: FOLoRA's training objective is
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}+\lambda\Omega$, so at the end of task $\mathcal{T}_\tau$ we have
$\nabla_\xi\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\xi_\tau)=-\lambda\nabla_\xi\Omega(\theta_\tau)$ rather
than exactly zero. We discuss the size of this deviation in
Remark~\ref{rem:regularized} and quantify it empirically in
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{A second-order forgetting bound}
\label{sec:bound}

\begin{theorem}[Second-order forgetting bound]
\label{thm:forgetting}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:stationary}--\ref{ass:lipschitz}, the forgetting of task
$\mathcal{T}_\tau$ after learning task $\mathcal{T}_t$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathcal{L}_\tau
\;\le\;
\frac12\,\delta\xi^{\tau\top} \mathcal{F}_\tau\,\delta\xi^{\tau}
\;+\;\frac{L_3}{6}\big\|\delta\xi^{\tau}\big\|^{3}
\;+\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}},
\label{eq:bound1}
\end{equation}
where
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}=\tfrac12\big|\delta\xi^{\tau\top}(H_\tau(\xi_\tau)-\mathcal{F}_\tau)\delta\xi^{\tau}\big|$
is the absolute error incurred by replacing the Hessian with the Fisher
(Assumption~\ref{ass:fisher}), and
$\mathcal{F}_\tau=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_\tau}[\,g_\xi g_\xi^{\!\top}]$,
with $g_\xi\equiv\nabla_\xi\log p_\xi(y|x)$, is the empirical Fisher of task $\tau$
over all trainable parameters.
% NOTE (2026-10-01): the expectation was previously written out in full here, twice
% over (gradient of the log-likelihood written out two times inside one inline
% formula). That single unbreakable inline math atom overflowed the 390pt review text
% block by 100pt -- i.e. 35mm of type into the margin. The shorthand $g_\xi$ is the
% same object the method section defines for the adapter block as $g_\theta$
% (Sec. 3), so the two now read as one notation rather than two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taylor-expanding $\mathcal{L}_\tau$ at $\xi_\tau$ along the displacement
$\delta\xi=\xi_t-\xi_\tau$ with the integral form of the remainder gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\tau(\xi_t)
= \mathcal{L}_\tau(\xi_\tau)
+ \nabla\mathcal{L}_\tau(\xi_\tau)^{\!\top}\delta\xi
+ \int_0^1 (1-s)\,\delta\xi^{\!\top} H_\tau(\xi_\tau+s\,\delta\xi)\,\delta\xi \,\mathrm{d}s .
\end{equation}
By Assumption~\ref{ass:stationary} the linear term vanishes. Decomposing the integral,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathcal{L}_\tau
= \frac12\,\delta\xi^{\!\top}H_\tau(\xi_\tau)\,\delta\xi
+ \int_0^1 (1-s)\,\delta\xi^{\!\top}\big[H_\tau(\xi_\tau{+}s\delta\xi)-H_\tau(\xi_\tau)\big]\delta\xi\,\mathrm{d}s .
\end{equation}
The integrand is bounded by
$(1-s)\,\|\delta\xi\|^2\,\|H_\tau(\xi_\tau{+}s\delta\xi)-H_\tau(\xi_\tau)\|_2
\le (1-s)\,s\,L_3\|\delta\xi\|^3$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz}, and
$\int_0^1 (1-s)s\,\mathrm{d}s = 1/6$, which bounds the remainder by
$\tfrac{L_3}{6}\|\delta\xi\|^3$. Substituting the Fisher approximation
(Assumption~\ref{ass:fisher}) into the leading term yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound1}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Isolating the adapter block}
Because $\xi=(\theta,\psi)$ is a concatenation, the leading term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound1} splits into an adapter block, a head block and a cross term. Writing
$\mathcal{F}_\tau$ in the same block structure,
\begin{equation}
\tfrac12\,\delta\xi^{\tau\top}\mathcal{F}_\tau\,\delta\xi^{\tau}
=
\underbrace{\tfrac12\,\delta\theta^{\tau\top} F_\tau\,\delta\theta^{\tau}}_{\text{adapter block}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\delta\theta^{\tau\top}\mathcal{F}_\tau^{\theta\psi}\delta\psi^{\tau}
+\tfrac12\,\delta\psi^{\tau\top}\mathcal{F}_\tau^{\psi\psi}\delta\psi^{\tau}}_{\text{head / cross terms}},
\quad
F_\tau := \mathcal{F}_\tau^{\theta\theta},
\label{eq:block}
\end{equation}
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound1} becomes
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathcal{L}_\tau
\;\le\;
\underbrace{\frac12\,\delta\theta^{\tau\top} F_\tau\,\delta\theta^{\tau}}_{\text{adapter term}}
\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{L_3}{6}\big\|\delta\xi^{\tau}\big\|^{3}}_{\text{higher order}}
\;+\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}
\;+\;R_{\text{head}},
\label{eq:bound_adapter}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\text{head}}$ collects the two head/cross terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:block} and
$F_\tau$ is the adapter block of $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ --- the empirical Fisher restricted to
the adapter parameters of one layer, of size $d\times d$. FOLoRA constrains the first term
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound_adapter} and nothing else; we carry $R_{\text{head}}$ explicitly
rather than dropping it, because the classifier head is trained between $\tau$ and $t$ and
its contribution to $\Delta\mathcal{L}_\tau$ is not zero.

Two approximations are made in \emph{estimating} $F_\tau$, as opposed to in the bound
itself, and we state both. First, the estimate is a rank-$k$ truncation, as described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:method}. Second, it is computed per adapted layer and treated as
block-diagonal across layers: correlations between the parameters of different adapted
layers are not modelled, so the $d\times d$ block above is one diagonal block of the full
adapter Fisher of dimension $L\,r(d_{\text{in}}+d_{\text{out}})$ for $L$ adapted layers.
Both are properties of the estimator; the bound is stated for the exact $F_\tau$, so
neither weakens Theorem~\ref{thm:forgetting}, but both are part of what a reader comparing
the paper to the released code should expect to find.

The decomposition is useful only if the adapter term dominates, which holds when the
displacement is small. In our setting this is not merely a hope: the adapter has a fixed
rank $r=16$ per layer and is trained under an explicit penalty on its displacement, so
$\|\delta\theta^{\tau}\|$ is bounded by the geometry of the problem rather than growing
with the number of tasks. Whether the higher-order term is in fact negligible at that
displacement is a question about the data, not about the algebra, and we measure it rather
than assume it: Sec.~\ref{sec:tightness} finds it to be the \emph{same order} as the
quadratic term, which is why Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound_adapter} is described throughout as an
upper bound with quantified slack rather than as a tight characterization of forgetting.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound_adapter} is a bound on the \emph{loss} of task $\tau$, and in that
bound the classifier head contributes through $R_{\text{head}}$. Our evaluation, however,
does not use the head: under the NCM protocol
(Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}) the head is discarded at test time and prediction is made by
nearest class mean in the frozen feature space, so the adapter is the \emph{only} pathway
by which a task's test accuracy can be affected after it has been learned. The bound's
scope and the evaluation protocol therefore coincide in the sense that matters for the
reported metric, even though they differ in the loss that the bound is stated for.

\begin{remark}[Scope: one reference point]
\label{rem:reference}
Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound_adapter} over all previous tasks $\tau<t$ requires a
displacement $\delta\theta^{\tau}$ \emph{per} task $\tau$, i.e.\ a snapshot of the adapter
parameters at every past task. FOLoRA stores a single reference $\theta^{t-1}$, following
the same single-reference approximation as EWC~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}, and
replaces $\delta\theta^{\tau}$ by $\delta\theta=\theta_t-\theta^{t-1}$. The induced error
is $\theta^{t-1}-\theta^{\tau}$, i.e.\ exactly the displacement accumulated between tasks
$\tau$ and $t-1$ --- which is itself penalized by the same regularizer at every
intermediate task. Writing $R$ for the head terms $R_{\text{head}}$ summed over $\tau$, the
bound we actually minimize is
% Split over two lines: on one line this display overflowed the 390pt review text
% block by 23.7pt (~8mm of math into the margin).
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\sum_{\tau<t}\Delta\mathcal{L}_\tau
\;\le\;
{}& \frac12\,\delta\theta^{\!\top}\Big(\sum_{\tau<t}F_\tau\Big)\delta\theta
\;+\;(t-1)\Big(\frac{L_3}{6}\|\delta\xi^{\max}\|^{3}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}\Big) \\
&+\;R
\;+\;\mathcal{O}\big(\|\theta^{t-1}-\theta^{\tau}\|^{2}\big),
\end{split}
\label{eq:sum}
\end{equation}
where we identify $\sum_{\tau<t}F_\tau$ with the accumulated importance kernel $\bar{F}$
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:acc}. The scope of the theorem is thus ``forgetting relative to the most
recent snapshot'', and the statement degrades gracefully rather than being exact for all
historical tasks.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Deviation from stationarity under the regularizer]
\label{rem:regularized}
Because FOLoRA minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}+\lambda\Omega$, the stationarity
Assumption~\ref{ass:stationary} holds only approximately, with residual gradient
$-\lambda\nabla_\xi\Omega(\theta_\tau)$ --- nonzero only in the adapter block, since
$\Omega$ does not depend on the head. Writing $\Omega(\delta\theta)=\sum_j\sigma_j
(u_j^{\!\top}\delta\theta)^2$ gives $\nabla_\theta\Omega(\theta_\tau)=2\bar{G}^{\!\top}\bar{G}\,\delta\theta$
where $\bar{G}$ stacks the accumulated Fisher directions. The residual therefore vanishes
proportionally to the displacement itself: in directions where protection is effective
($\delta\theta\!\to\!0$) the assumption is self-consistently satisfied, while in weakly
protected directions it degrades linearly in $\lambda$. This provides a theoretical
counterweight to increasing $\lambda$ beyond the range where the displacement is small,
and is consistent with the ablation in Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}: at $k{=}16$, where the
penalty acts on fewer directions, average accuracy peaks at $\lambda\in\{3,10\}$ and then
declines through $\lambda=100$ before saturating by $\lambda=300$. At
$k{=}64$ the same decline is not visible over the range we probed, and we do not claim it
there.
\end{remark}

\subsection{FOLoRA minimizes the bound}
\label{sec:minimize}

Using the eigendecomposition $\bar{F}=\sum_j\sigma_j u_j u_j^{\!\top}$, the leading term
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sum} becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac12\,\delta\theta^{\!\top}\bar{F}\,\delta\theta
\;=\;
\frac12\sum_{j}\sigma_j\,\big(u_j^{\!\top}\delta\theta\big)^2 ,
\label{eq:eigenbound}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the adapter-parameter displacement and each
$u_j\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is a unit eigenvector of $\bar{F}$, so every inner product above is
between vectors of the same dimension.
% NOTE (2026-09-24): convention pin-down. Throughout this section $\sigma_j$ is an
% *eigenvalue* of $\bar F$, i.e. $\sigma_j=s_j^2$ where $s_j$ is the corresponding
% singular value of the per-sample gradient matrix. The implementation's own docstrings
% use $\sigma_j$ for the *singular value* $s_j$ and report the penalty weight as
% $\sigma_j^2$; both describe the same object, but a reader comparing code to paper
% without this sentence will conclude the paper misstates its own regularizer. Keep it.
Equation~\eqref{eq:eigenbound} shows that the dominant term of the forgetting bound is a
sum of squared projections of the displacement onto the eigendirections of the
accumulated Fisher, weighted by their importance $\sigma_j$. Here $\sigma_j$ denotes an
eigenvalue of $\bar{F}$; the implementation stores each direction scaled by the
corresponding singular value $s_j=\sqrt{\sigma_j}$ of the per-sample gradient matrix, so
that the squared norm it penalizes carries weight $\sigma_j$ exactly as written. The
FOLoRA regularizer in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss},
\begin{equation}
\Omega(\delta\theta)=\sum_j\sigma_j\,\big(u_j^{\!\top}\delta\theta\big)^2,
\qquad \delta\theta=\theta-\theta^{t-1},
\end{equation}
% NOTE (2026-09-24): this sentence previously claimed $\Omega$ "is precisely the
% Lagrangian relaxation of the constraint that the adapter displacement remain
% orthogonal to the high-importance eigendirections". That is NOT true: relaxing a
% hard orthogonality constraint gives a term $\sum_j\mu_j(u_j^\top\delta\theta)$ with free
% multipliers $\mu_j$, not fixed weights $\sigma_j$. The correct referent is the
% *ellipsoid* constraint, for which $\Omega$ is exactly the Lagrangian penalty.
% Do not revert to the orthogonality wording -- a theory reviewer will reject it.
is, up to the factor $2$, exactly the penalty term of the Lagrangian relaxation of the
constraint that the leading term of the bound remain below a tolerance $\epsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta^{\!\top}\bar{F}\,\delta\theta\;\le\;\epsilon ,
\end{equation}
since relaxing that constraint with multiplier $\lambda/2$ returns
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}+\lambda\Omega$. Minimizing $\Omega$ therefore drives
$|u_j^{\!\top}\delta\theta|$ toward zero for the largest $\sigma_j$, which in turn minimizes
the dominant terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:eigenbound}. We therefore interpret FOLoRA as the
algorithm that \emph{directly minimizes the leading term of a second-order bound on
catastrophic forgetting}, with $\lambda$ controlling the stability--plasticity trade-off
and $k$ controlling the rank of the protected subspace.

\subsection{How tight is the bound?}
\label{sec:tightness}

Theorem~\ref{thm:forgetting} is an upper bound, and everything above uses only its
\emph{structure}. This subsection reports what the bound's own terms evaluate to on a
trained run, because two of the approximations it inherits turn out to be violated by
amounts that change how the bound should be read.

All numbers below come from an in-training probe that, at each task boundary of a 20-task
run, fits the expansion of the accumulated past-task loss along the \emph{realized}
adapter displacement,
$\Delta\mathcal{L}(\alpha\,\delta\theta)=a\alpha+b\alpha^{2}+c\alpha^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})$,
over $\alpha\in[0,1.5]$. Measuring the nonlinearity along the realized displacement, rather
than bounding $L_3$, is what makes the remainder checkable at all: $L_3$ is not recoverable
from any stored checkpoint, whereas $a$, $b$ and $c$ are. We report $a$ and $c$ as ratios
to $|b|$, so that ``is this correction small?'' is read directly off the number. The probe
run is FOLoRA at $\lambda{=}10$, $k{=}16$, seed~0 on Split CIFAR-100, giving 19 usable
boundaries. It is a single configuration, and we do not have the seed spread needed to say
how much of what follows is run-to-run variation; the numbers should be read as one
operating point, not as a distribution over the method.

\paragraph{(i) The remainder is not negligible}
The ratio $|c|/|b|$ has median $0.163$, minimum $0.010$ and maximum $2.46$, with $5$ of the
$19$ boundaries above $0.30$. Under the decision rule frozen before these numbers were
computed (median $\le 0.10$: keep the negligibility claim; $0.10<\text{median}\le 0.30$:
weaken it; $>0.30$: drop it), this lands in the middle band, and the honest reading is the
one the frozen rule prescribes: the cubic term is \emph{of the same order as, but does not
dominate}, the quadratic term. The tail is the part that matters --- at the worst boundary
the quantity the bound calls a remainder is $2.5\times$ the quantity FOLoRA minimizes.

\paragraph{(ii) The stationarity residual is not small either}
The ratio $|a|/|b|$ has median $0.99$ and maximum $23.1$.
Assumption~\ref{ass:stationary} is stated for the \emph{unregularized} loss, so at a
regularized endpoint the residual is $-\lambda\nabla\Omega$, and
Remark~\ref{rem:regularized} argued that it should vanish proportionally to the
displacement. It does not vanish fast enough to be dropped: at the realized displacement
the linear term is comparable to the quadratic one, so the ``quadratic plus corrections''
reading of the expansion is not numerically available at this $\lambda$.

\paragraph{(iii) The Fisher estimate is not a calibrated surrogate for the curvature}
The ratio between the term the bound prescribes, $\frac12\delta\theta^{\!\top}\bar{F}\delta\theta$,
and the quadratic coefficient $b$ fitted from the same measurement has median
$2.8\times10^{-5}$: the finite-sample, rank-$k$, block-diagonal estimate of $F_\tau$
underestimates the measured curvature of the past loss along the realized displacement by
four to five orders of magnitude.
It is worth being precise about what this does and does not say. It does \emph{not}
contradict Theorem~\ref{thm:forgetting}, which is stated for the exact $F_\tau$; it says
that Assumption~\ref{ass:fisher} is far from innocuous at this operating point, which is
precisely the regime the empirical-Fisher literature warns about~\cite{kunstner2019limitations}.
It is also, in part, the intended behaviour seen from the other side: the displacement is
close to orthogonal to the top-$k$ Fisher subspace \emph{by construction}, since suppressing
that alignment is what the penalty does, so the Fisher-weighted quadratic form is small
along it. Both readings agree on the operative point: the bound's leading term is not a
quantitative predictor of forgetting in this setting.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
The bound is used here as a \emph{design principle}. It identifies which quantity a
regularizer should suppress --- the curvature-weighted projection of the adapter
displacement onto the past tasks' protected directions --- and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eigenbound} shows that FOLoRA's penalty is exactly the Lagrangian form of
that quantity. We make no claim that minimizing it produces a predictable reduction in
forgetting, and Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments} does not test such a claim. The empirical case
for FOLoRA is made by ablation instead, and it is a clean one: removing $\Omega$ from the
objective and changing nothing else takes Split CIFAR-100 accuracy from $78.32$ to $69.59$
($n{=}5$, paired $p=0.0005$, negative in all five seeds), i.e.\ down to the level of
unprotected sequential fine-tuning ($69.31$, $p=0.53$). The entire effect of the method is
carried by the term the bound identifies, which is the sense in which the derivation is
load-bearing --- as motivation for \emph{what} to penalize, not as a guarantee of \emph{how
much} it helps.

\subsection{Relation to prior forgetting bounds}
\label{sec:prior_bounds}

Second-order analyses of catastrophic forgetting are well established for models trained
from scratch. Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2019toward} show that forgetting is driven by the
alignment between successive tasks' loss landscapes and derive a worst-case bound in
terms of the curvature of the old task; Evron et al.~\cite{evron2022catastrophic} give
tight bounds for linear networks showing that forgetting is governed by the overlap
between the old task's input distribution and the directions in which the new task
changes the weights; Karakida and Akaho~\cite{karakida2022learning} analyse learning
curves for continual learning using NTK overlap matrices; and
Doan et al.~\cite{doan2021theoretical} quantify forgetting through the NTK overlap between
tasks. Closer in spirit, orthogonal-gradient
descent~\cite{bennani2019generalisation,farajtabar2018orthogonal} admits generalization
bounds obtained by constraining updates to the null space of previous task gradients.

Our analysis differs from this line in where it is instantiated. Prior results bound
forgetting for \emph{full} parameter updates of models trained from scratch, and are used
mainly to explain forgetting. We instantiate the bound in the \emph{low-rank adapter
subspace} of a frozen pre-trained backbone, where the displacement is supported on a
$r(d_{\text{in}}\!+\!d_{\text{out}})$-dimensional subspace per layer, and we use the
bound constructively: its dominant term is exactly the quantity a subspace-regularizer
can minimize, which yields the FOLoRA objective. The resulting penalty differs from
null-space projection~\cite{saha2021gradient,bennani2019generalisation} in being
importance-weighted and soft rather than hard, so that the fixed rank budget is spent on
the directions with the largest curvature.

\subsection{Connection to equal-weight orthogonalization}
\label{sec:equalweight}

If all eigenvalues are set to $\sigma_j=1$, the leading term of the bound,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eigenbound}, becomes $\frac12\|\bar{U}^{\!\top}\delta\theta\|^2$, where
$\bar{U}=[u_1,\dots,u_k]$ is the orthonormal basis of the retained protected subspace ---
the equal-weight geometry underlying O-LoRA-style orthogonal subspace methods. The
eigenvalue-weighted family therefore contains the equal-weight geometry as the special case
$\sigma_j\equiv1$, and the two coincide exactly when the spectrum is flat over the
protected subspace; on our checkpoints it is not flat, so the weighted family is the
strictly larger one as a family of penalties.

A larger family is not by itself an accuracy claim, and one tempting over-claim is worth
pre-empting here: because every coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eigenbound} is non-negative,
weighting by $\sigma_j$ does \emph{not} reduce the value of the bound at a fixed
displacement -- the weighted coefficients can exceed $1$. The claim is the weaker and
well-defined one that the weighted penalty attains a \emph{lower value of the bound after
both objectives are optimized} for a given protected rank $k$, by spending the fixed rank
budget where the curvature is largest. Whether that is realized in practice is an
empirical question; Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation} characterizes the weighted family over
$\lambda$ and $k$, and the equal-weight special case $\sigma_j\equiv1$ is its natural
control.
