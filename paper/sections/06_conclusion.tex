\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We propose FOLoRA, a replay-free during training method for continual learning that mitigates catastrophic
forgetting in low-rank adaptation while keeping the number of trainable parameters fixed
as the number of tasks grows. FOLoRA maintains an accumulated
second-order importance kernel for each adapted layer, forms an importance-weighted
protected subspace from its top eigendirections, and penalizes alignment of the adapter's
displacement with those directions in proportion to their importance. We derive a
forgetting upper bound that decomposes into Fisher-weighted subspace overlaps and showed
that FOLoRA's regularizer minimizes its dominant term, thereby providing a principled
stability--plasticity trade-off and containing equal-weight orthogonalization as a special
case. We also measure the slack of that bound instead of assuming it away: at the
operating point we probe the remainder and the stationarity residual are the same order as
the quadratic term, so the bound is presented as a design principle for what to penalize
rather than as a tight or numerically predictive characterization of forgetting. The
empirical case for the regularizer rests on the $\lambda{=}0$ ablation, which removes the
Fisher-weighted penalty while changing nothing else and returns the method to the level of
unprotected sequential fine-tuning. Experiments on standard class-incremental benchmarks show that FOLoRA reaches an
average accuracy on par with the strongest regularized baseline while using no replay during
training and no growth in the parameter count, and we quantify the importance-model memory
it pays for representing that model as a rank-$k$ subspace rather than EWC's diagonal one
--- $32.5\times$ EWC's importance state at $k{=}64$ and $8.5\times$ at $k{=}16$, which is the
trade-off that makes $k$ a memory--accuracy dial rather than a free parameter. When that memory
is the binding constraint, $k{=}16$ at $\lambda\in\{3,10\}$ is the setting to use: on CIFAR-100
it is within one point of the main configuration with no significant difference ($p\ge0.27$),
on ImageNet-R within $1.1$ points with no significant difference either, and it costs $47.8$
instead of $182.8$\,MiB. We also
report the run-to-run reproducibility floor we measured (a single-run standard deviation of
$0.63$ points, i.e.\ $0.89$ points for the difference of two runs at a fixed seed and a
fixed configuration, from eleven repeated runs), and we state the resulting conclusions at the strength the data
supports: parity with the strongest regularized baseline, rather than an improvement over
it, and a significant advantage over the prompt and subspace baselines, with the O-LoRA
comparison significant on CIFAR-100 and significant on the gap metric on both benchmarks.

The Fisher weighting itself is not what produces the margin. A tuned isotropic displacement
penalty of matched RMS, which protects no direction in particular, reaches the same accuracy
as FOLoRA on both benchmarks, and its CIL--TIL gap differs by at most a quarter point ---
nominally smaller on CIFAR-100, nominally larger on ImageNet-R --- and the difference in what
the two penalties protect does not show up in either metric at this budget. What the experiments support is
therefore narrower than the mechanism the method is named after: a shared adapter protected
by an orthogonality penalty matches the strongest regularized baseline and beats the
per-task-module family, and the choice of weight inside the penalty is not what separates
them. We state this because it is the result, and because it has a practical consequence ---
where memory is binding, the isotropic penalty needs one scalar per layer in place of the
$182.8$\,MiB the rank-$k$ basis retains, and is the better choice for the same accuracy.

A natural direction for future work is to extend FOLoRA to task-agnostic settings with
distribution shift (domain-incremental learning) and to language models, where the
low-rank subspace geometry of different task families can be exploited more explicitly.
