\title{FOLoRA: Fisher-Orthogonalized Low-Rank Adaptation for Continual Learning}

\begin{highlights}
\item FOLoRA protects the Fisher-important directions of a shared LoRA adapter.
\item Fisher weighting sets the operating strength, not the accuracy ceiling.
\item FOLoRA minimizes the leading term of a second-order bound with explicit remainder.
\item One setting transfers across benchmarks; EWC-LoRA's default strength does not.
\item Beats L2P, CODA-Prompt, O-LoRA and InfLoRA on both benchmarks; no module per task.
\end{highlights}

\author[hufe]{Jiang Tao\corref{cor1}}
\ead{jiangtao.ml@foxmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}
\address[hufe]{School of Intelligent Information Engineering, Heilongjiang University of Finance and Economics, Harbin, China}

\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as low-rank adaptation (LoRA)
freeze a pre-trained backbone and train few parameters, suiting continual learning
on resource-constrained devices. However, sequentially fine-tuning a shared LoRA
adapter still forgets catastrophically: the low-rank subspace is overwritten by new
tasks. We propose \textbf{FOLoRA} (Fisher-Orthogonalized Low-Rank Adaptation), a
replay-free, fixed-budget method protecting the directions past tasks deem important.
FOLoRA accumulates, per adapted layer, a second-order importance kernel from the
empirical Fisher on adapter parameters. Its top eigendirections form
an \emph{importance-weighted protected subspace}, and a regularizer penalizes alignment
of the adapter displacement with them, weighted by eigenvalue.
We further derive a second-order forgetting bound with an explicit remainder and show
FOLoRA's regularizer minimizes its leading term, using it as a design principle rather
than a numerical predictor.
Under a common frozen-feature nearest-class-mean decision rule, FOLoRA shows no significant
difference from the strongest tuned EWC-LoRA configuration on either benchmark; its positive
result is against the parameter-efficient family, where it
improves significantly over L2P, CODA-Prompt, O-LoRA and InfLoRA on both benchmarks while
allocating no module per task, so its footprint stays constant in the task count
($\sim18\times$ smaller than a per-task adapter bank), and it needs no replay, adds no
inference cost and merges into the backbone. One setting is used unchanged on both
benchmarks, where EWC-LoRA's pre-registered default strength does not transfer. Only the
classifier head grows with the task count, shared by all methods and discarded at
evaluation. The trade-off is a larger importance-state footprint than EWC's diagonal
approximation.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
The datasets used in this study (CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R) are publicly available
standard continual-learning benchmarks. The source code implementing the proposed method,
all baselines, the evaluation protocol and the scripts that reproduce every table and
figure in this paper are openly available at
\url{https://github.com/ml-universe/FOLoRA}. The repository ships the code, the
configuration files, and the evaluation outputs under \texttt{reports/} from which the
result tables are generated, so every reported number can be re-derived without
retraining; it does not redistribute the benchmark datasets or the trained checkpoints,
which are large and are regenerated by the training scripts.
