\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as low-rank adaptation (LoRA)
freeze a pre-trained backbone and learn only a small number of trainable parameters,
making them attractive for continual learning on resource-constrained devices.
However, even with few trainable parameters, sequentially fine-tuning a shared LoRA
adapter across tasks still suffers from catastrophic forgetting, because the low-rank
subspace is repeatedly overwritten by new tasks. In this paper we propose
\textbf{FOLoRA} (Fisher-Orthogonalized Low-Rank Adaptation), a replay-free,
fixed-budget method that mitigates forgetting by protecting the directions that past
tasks deem important. FOLoRA maintains, for each adapted layer, an accumulated
second-order importance kernel estimated from the empirical Fisher information restricted
to the adapter parameters. Before learning a new task, the top eigendirections of this
kernel form an \emph{importance-weighted protected subspace}; during training, a
regularizer penalizes the alignment of the adapter displacement with these directions,
weighted by their eigenvalues. This yields a principled stability--plasticity trade-off:
strongly important directions are preserved, while unimportant directions remain
available for reuse. We further derive a second-order bound on the forgetting of a
previously learned task that carries an explicit higher-order remainder, and show that
FOLoRA's regularizer minimizes its leading term. We audit the slack of that bound
empirically rather than assuming it away, and use it as a design principle for
\emph{what} to penalize rather than as a numerical predictor of \emph{how much} it helps. Experiments on Split CIFAR-100 and Split
ImageNet-R under the standard frozen-feature nearest-class-mean protocol show that FOLoRA
reaches an average accuracy on par with the strongest regularized baseline. Unlike
per-task-adapter methods such as O-LoRA and InfLoRA, FOLoRA requires neither replay nor any
growth in parameter count with the number of tasks---about $18\times$ fewer trainable
parameters---and adds no inference cost, since the low-rank update merges back into the
frozen backbone. We also characterize the resulting trade-off: because FOLoRA represents
its importance model as a rank-$k$ subspace rather than EWC's full diagonal, it pays a
larger importance-model footprint, which we quantify alongside the accuracy comparison.
\end{abstract}
