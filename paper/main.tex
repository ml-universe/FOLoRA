\begin{highlights}
\item FOLoRA protects the Fisher-important directions of a shared LoRA adapter.
\item Protected directions are weighted by Fisher importance, not protected equally.
\item FOLoRA minimizes the leading term of a second-order bound with explicit remainder.
\item Fixed trainable-parameter budget with no replay and no inference overhead.
\item One setting transfers across benchmarks; EWC-LoRA's default strength does not.

\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as low-rank adaptation (LoRA)
freeze a pre-trained backbone and train few parameters, suiting continual learning
on resource-constrained devices. However, sequentially fine-tuning a shared LoRA
adapter still forgets catastrophically: the low-rank subspace is overwritten by new
tasks. We propose \textbf{FOLoRA} (Fisher-Orthogonalized Low-Rank Adaptation), a
replay-free, fixed-budget method protecting the directions past tasks deem important.
FOLoRA accumulates, per adapted layer, a second-order importance kernel from the
empirical Fisher on the adapter parameters. Its top eigendirections form
an \emph{importance-weighted protected subspace}, and a regularizer penalizes alignment
of the adapter displacement with them, weighted by eigenvalue.
This yields a stability--plasticity trade-off between preserved and reusable directions.
We further derive a second-order forgetting bound with an explicit remainder and show
FOLoRA's regularizer minimizes its leading term; we audit that bound's slack empirically,
using it as a design principle rather than a numerical predictor.
Under the frozen-feature nearest-class-mean protocol, FOLoRA
matches the strongest regularized baseline on Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet-R;
one setting is used unchanged on both, where EWC-LoRA's pre-registered default strength
does not transfer. Unlike per-task-adapter methods
(O-LoRA, InfLoRA), FOLoRA needs no replay and no new module per task
($\sim18\times$ fewer trainable parameters), adds no inference cost, and merges into the
backbone; only the classifier head grows with the task count, shared by all methods and
discarded at evaluation under this protocol. We also quantify the trade-off: a rank-$k$
importance model rather than EWC's diagonal costs a larger footprint.
\end{abstract}

all baselines, the evaluation protocol and the scripts that reproduce every table and
figure in this paper are openly available at

configuration files, and the evaluation outputs under \texttt{reports/} from which the
result tables are generated, so every reported number can be re-derived without
retraining; it does not redistribute the benchmark datasets or the trained checkpoints,
which are large and are regenerated by the training scripts.
